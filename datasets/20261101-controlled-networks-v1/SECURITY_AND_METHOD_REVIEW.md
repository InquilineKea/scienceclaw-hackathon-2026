# Execution and model review — 1 November 2026

This study uses newly authored Python standard-library code, not imports, tests, builds or commands from the ScienceClaw repository. No new dependency, package or installer is involved. The local third-party execution approval gate is therefore not invoked. Existing framework Python is run with `-I -S`; no user site-packages load. Residual risk: the preinstalled interpreter/OS runtime has not been independently rebuilt or exhaustively verified.

`source/compare.py` has file access limited to output arguments and no network, process launch, environment inspection or dynamic code evaluation. `source/launch.py` starts this script under macOS deny-default sandbox restrictions, sanitized HOME/TMPDIR and a 1,200-second timeout. Its recorded probes confirm private file reads and network connection attempts are denied. No credentials enter the scientific worker. Publishing is a later host-side action using the existing legitimate GitHub connection. The first reference-test attempt stopped before scientific cases because the energy-gradient check mistakenly selected a clamped node; its exact source and diagnostic remain preserved. The corrected check selects a free node.

The scientific implementation is a deliberately limited, original small-strain central-force brittle truss, not a reproduction of the nonlinear atlas or FEniCSx. Edges retain reference directions. Energy is sum(0.5 k [n dot (u_b-u_a)]^2). Free displacements minimize this energy under clamped top/bottom grips. Top grip moves axially for tension or transversely for held-out shear; other grip degrees remain fixed. k=A/L, E=1. A=1/sum(L) gives unit initial fiber material measure in each dimension. All edges break irreversibly when absolute linearized axial strain exceeds their seeded uniform [0.064, 0.096] threshold; compression rupture is an imposed rule, not collagen buckling. Each avalanche re-equilibrates at fixed displacement. All removed-edge identities, thresholds, geometry, orientations and solve logs are saved.

Three architecture generators use unit-height, approximately unit-width specimens: a 49-node triangular topology (staggered rows stretched to a square bounding region); a 63-node FCC topology; and the same FCC topology with larger positional disorder. The disordered family is geometrically disordered FCC, not a general random fibrous graph or an independent connectivity ensemble. Small internal jitter supplies ten geometry realizations per family, seeds 101–110. Orientation and node/edge counts are recorded; they are measured covariates, not matched controls. Surface-to-volume and node counts differ between 2D/3D; no raw dimensional stresses or energies are pooled. This is not a finite-size convergence study or a test of arbitrary connectivities.

Intact, clustered-central and uniformly sampled distributed removal use the same geometry and thresholds within each seed. The two damage protocols remove equal edge counts (rounded 10%); removed material measure and mean distance from grips are recorded but not exactly matched. Geometry family, orientation and coordination are not independently manipulated. Those remain limitations on causal interpretation of damage placement.

Equal-material and matched-intact-axial-stiffness results are reported separately. All stiffnesses within one intact geometry are uniformly rescaled by 1/K_axial to obtain K_axial=1. Damage is applied after this common scaling, so damaged specimens are not rematched. Shear is held out from the fit and deliberately need not have equal shear stiffness. Uniform scaling exactly scales forces, energies and work, and leaves displacements and strain-triggered break events unchanged in this model; both normalizations share raw trajectories rather than pretending they are independently computed experiments. The matched scale can be interpreted as changing modulus at fixed geometry/material measure; it does not provide a physically matched chemical composition.

Operational failure: remaining secant stiffness below 10% of that pattern's initial stiffness. Stop at that event or imposed strain 0.24; absence of failure is right-censored. Loading is quasistatic normalized strain, not physical time, pressure, acceleration or relaxation. Curves use increments 0.01 and 0.005; equilibrium tolerances are 1e-9 and 1e-10 with explicit recomputed residual gate max(1e-7,100*tol). Convergence failures are retained. Quadrature for external work is a finite-step trapezoid across brittle jumps and is subject to refinement rejection; no calibrated fracture-energy claim follows. Small reference checks compare CG against separately implemented dense elimination, an analytic series-spring solution and a free-node energy gradient. These do not independently validate the full architecture/fracture implementation.

No viscoelastic relaxation, transient energy balance, hydrodynamic pressure, recruitment, crosslink reactions, species strength or tissue calibration is simulated. In particular this is a bounded geometry control, not completion of every gate in the prior six-point falsification protocol. Future time-dependent and nonlinear comparisons must be distinct runs.

FEniCSx figures reanalyze the preserved `web/comparisons.csv` and `validation.json`, with source SHA-256; they do not rerun FEniCSx or its plotting dependencies. Only rows with the original accepted `resolved` flag enter the effect scatter, and all 1,600 rows remain in the filterable table. Mesh gates and denominators are shown separately.

## Event-resolved follow-up

The completed first sweep found 51/180 work comparisons with >5% increment sensitivity and 13 changed censoring outcomes, despite converged equilibrium solves. It is retained as a numerical diagnostic and is not the primary result. `source/event_followup.py` then executed 180 new event-resolved cases and 18 stricter-tolerance controls in the same sandbox. The next load is the minimum remaining edge threshold divided by its unit-displacement strain, capped at 0.24. Work between events is exactly 0.5 K (epsilon_next² - epsilon_previous²). Avalanches occur at fixed displacement; elastic energy released by each removal is accumulated separately. This avoids stepping over failure thresholds and supplies an external-work = stored-energy + released-energy check. It does not add physical fracture calibration or finite-deformation mechanics. Matching and geometry/damage caveats above still apply. The 18 controls cover seed 101 for every family/pattern/loading combination; tighter-tolerance checks were not performed for all ten seeds. The primary event-resolved quantities supersede the initial finite-step diagnostic for conclusions, while every earlier raw file remains available.
