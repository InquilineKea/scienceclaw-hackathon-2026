# Mechanistic transition atlas — revised simulation scope

This is a new, separate version. Original finished atlas folders and the earlier molecular runs remain unchanged. All network lengths, stresses, strains, rates and kinetic times are dimensionless; area fraction in two dimensions is not tissue volume fraction. The simulations are mechanistic hypotheses, not calibrated collagen, lung, cancer, or species predictions.

## What the critique changes

1. **Strain-driven rigidity.** Finite, diluted periodic triangular fibre networks are sheared from γ=0 to 0.8. One bond per periodic fibre line is removed before dilution. Connectivity, bending stiffness, size and seed vary; every sampled equilibrium records its force residual, energy change, tangent shear modulus and incremental nonaffinity. A peak at a sampling boundary is not an identified critical strain. Finite-size crossovers do not establish critical exponents. Graph Laplacian statistics are saved alongside outcomes, without treating one scalar as a universal predictor. Reference: Sharma et al. (2016), DOI 10.1038/nphys3628; Jansen et al. (2018), DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2018.04.043.

2. **Sliding versus locking.** The same initial spring moduli, graph and rupture thresholds are compared across a hypothetical locking input. It changes mobility as 2 exp(-6 locking). Positive rest-length growth moves toward length/(1+yield strain), with yield strain 0.025, and is integrated exponentially. The fixed-coordinate energy decrement is recorded as dissipation. Load–hold–unload curves, a zero-stress residual-strain root, tensile rupture and removed fracture energy are saved. The locking input is not a measured AGE dose, and no chemical reaction is simulated. References: Fessel et al. (2014), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0110948; Gautieri et al. (2017), DOI 10.1016/j.matbio.2016.09.001.

3. **Stiffening and damage.** Existing bonds are stiffened ×20 in random or spatially clustered order; graph connectivity is unchanged. Consequently this is a stiffening crossover, not automatically a rigidity-percolation transition. A separate adaptive high-tension-cut versus random-cut experiment extends damage to 65% of initial bonds. This does not model lung alveolar geometry or patient disease progression. Reference: Bates et al. (2007), DOI 10.1164/rccm.200611-1739OC.

4. **Inclusion contact under compression.** Polydisperse disks at network junctions add an explicit finite-range overlap energy, 50 × overlap², using periodic nearest images. Area fraction varies from 0 to 0.84; uniaxial compression extends to 0.45. This is a centre-connected fibre/inclusion surrogate, not an anatomically resolved cell-laden gel. Initial contact/prestress is recorded rather than assumed absent. The zero-radius control has exactly zero contact energy. References: van Oosten et al. (2019), DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1516-5; Shivers et al. (2020), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2003037117.

5. **Plastic yielding and memory.** A separate amplitude × hold-time × locking matrix records stress relaxation and the zero-stress crossing during unloading. Irreversible fibre rest-length growth is a phenomenological mechanism; explicit junction unbinding/rebinding and fibre merging are not resolved. Therefore agreement with the direction of a published effect is not a reproduction of that experiment. References: Kim et al. (2017), DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-01011-7; Nam et al. (2016), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1523906113.

6. **Thermal sensitivity and its validation boundary.** An exact 81-site, two-state transfer matrix measures folded-site fraction versus dimensionless temperature under declared Hyp/hydration/crosslink sensitivity inputs. Site stabilization is 0.8+0.25Hyp+0.15hydration+0.10crosslink; boundary penalty is 0.45+0.25crosslink; folding entropy cost is 1. These coefficients are hypotheses, not force-field parameters or fitted measurements. There is no Celsius conversion and no avian-versus-human prediction. THERMAL_MD_GATE.json records the missing chemistry and equilibrium-sampling requirements for actual melting MD. Human monomer unfolding and chicken keel-bone calorimetry are not directly interchangeable assays. References: Leikina et al. (2002), DOI 10.1073/pnas.032307099; Losso and Ogawa (2014), DOI 10.1111/jfbc.12059.

7. **LOX–contractility feedback.** A hypothetical normalized model uses dS/dt=0.25+aL−S, dC/dt=S⁴/(1+S⁴)−C, and dL/dt=0.05+gC−L. All equilibria found by bracketing are checked using the Jacobian eigenvalues; two stable roots separated by an unstable root are required to call the mathematical model bistable. Increasing and decreasing gain paths test hysteresis independently. This extends the test beyond a single loop-gain number; the basal terms and gains are not fitted to LOX biology. References: Levental et al. (2009), DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2009.10.027; Ronceray et al. (2016), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1514208113.

## Numerical and storage rules

Float64 PyTorch L-BFGS minimizes the summed batch energy. Fresh per-system gradients and energy changes are checked after every minimization round. Every curve retains its convergence flag; invalid points remain in raw data and are not eligible for conclusions. Plotted gaps indicate invalid samples. Peak nonaffinity and apparent cut thresholds are finite-resolution descriptors. Neighbouring validity is required when interpreting a numerical derivative.

The initial check suite includes an analytic triangular-lattice shear modulus, a force finite difference, rigid-motion invariance, zero-mobility and zero-radius controls, dissipation sign, feedback stability fields, bounded transfer-matrix probabilities and CPU/GPU energy agreement. Saved states represent protocol samples; internal optimizer iterates are not claimed to have been recorded. Each output directory is allocated exclusively, sealed with complete SHA-256 manifests, and retained after failure or timeout. Publication uses the existing append-only GitHub/Release workflow.
