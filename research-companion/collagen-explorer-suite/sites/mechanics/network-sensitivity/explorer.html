Does clustering still change adhesion forces when sliding physics changes?

The direction is conditional. The same initial stiffness can coexist with lower or higher clustered-network adhesion forces when the relative sliding time changes. Changing the exact law family alone produced no direction-resolved sign reversals at fixed tested parameters.

Validation and limits

Initial shear energy matches to2×10⁻¹⁵ relative error; maximum equilibrium residual is1×10⁻¹⁰. Force-energy derivatives, rigid translation, force balance, nonnegative sliding dissipation and the exact unmodified-label null passed. Halving the time step changed hold-force integrals by at most0.341%, and scenario-mean contrasts by at most0.0265 percentage points in the checked subset.

The larger-domain test changed topology effects considerably, while also changing cluster footprint and geometry; it is not mesh convergence. A separately documented nested-core check preserves the original local graph and selected edges while adding surrounding matrix. The preserved-core check changed scenario means by −2.01 to +0.79 percentage points in the tested subset, with intervals for all changes spanning zero; it did not reproduce the large shift from changing the geometry and footprint.
