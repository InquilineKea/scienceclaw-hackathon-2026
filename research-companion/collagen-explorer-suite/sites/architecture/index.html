What is being modeled? Each fiber is a curved chain with its own stress-free contour, axial stretch energy, preferred-angle bending energy and a regularized twist penalty. Internal points and junctions relax in three dimensions at successive load steps. The x test constrains transverse dimensions. Changing crimp changes actual contour length; preserving material budget adjusts cross-section accordingly. Moving a reference junction rebuilds the unstressed fiber geometry. This is a nonlinear axial test, not the previous linear stiffness-tensor calculation.
Calibration, assumptions and what “better” means

Compare higher x stiffness against lower local strain concentration at equal total nominal fiber material, within a geometry family. Geometry families have different connectivity and candidate density. A single winner need not exist. The default graph is tiny and needs size, orientation and seed refinement. Crimp, bending and nonlinear geometry are now represented. A phenomenological twist penalty has rigidity 0.5 times bending rigidity, with a smooth normal regularizer of 0.01 times local segment length squared. These choices have not been fitted to collagen. Sliding, rupture, contact, fluid transport and fatigue remain absent.

A separate measured bovine hydrogel dataset anchors an example bulk storage modulus of 9.522 Pa at 1.5 mg/mL. Its prior held-out normalized error is about 0.498. That anchor does not identify fiber parameters or calibrate these tensile predictions. This editor therefore shows normalized mechanics. It does not predict human tendon strength, aging or gene expression.
