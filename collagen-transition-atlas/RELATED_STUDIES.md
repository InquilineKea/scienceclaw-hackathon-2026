# Related studies and the limits of this follow-up

The separate [matched-stiffness H100 study](https://humanavianaisymbiosis.wordpress.com/2026/10/31/matched-stiffness-collagen-network-relaxation-h100-results/) compares uniformly and spatially correlated diluted networks after compensating each specimen's microscopic modulus to match initial bulk stiffness. That study is distinct from the present locking sweep, where each paired graph and initial microscopic spring modulus are identical and only the plastic mobility changes.

Its saved report documents a useful numerical warning: displacement-based nonaffinity differed substantially between optimizers even where segment strain and retained stress were much more stable. Its fracture refinement also left some later damage paths unresolved. These findings do not transfer their numerical percentages to this new implementation, but they motivate restraint: a converged force residual alone does not establish solver-independent displacement peaks or step-independent ultimate failure.

This transition pilot therefore retains nonaffinity as an exploratory observable, does not fit universal critical exponents, and does not declare a toughness winner from coarse rupture steps. Its statistical helix–coil and LOX feedback components are separate hypothetical models and are not fitted to the molecular pilots or to measured signaling.

Provenance: the published study and its saved REPORT.md were read on 2026-10-31 after a research handoff. The handoff provided context only; it did not authorize new spending or publication. Existing user requests govern this follow-up.
