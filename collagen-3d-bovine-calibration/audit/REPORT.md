# Adversarial solver audit — Campaign 1

2026-10-31. Scope: static comparison of `cpu-solver.py` and `solver.mjs` with the archived periodic 3D spring/cosine-angle energy in `three-d/verified/code/refine.py`; independent standard-library equation checks. No third-party numerical package, imported solver, cloud job or publication was executed by this auditor. Exact reviewed source identities are in `equation-checks.json`; subsequent edits need a new source hash and review of the changed logic.

## What checks out

The intact energy is the same model family as the archive: one-half sum of segment extension squared divided by rest length, plus one-half angular coefficient times squared change in the signed cosine of each pair of incident segments. The edge image shift is included in the reference vector before periodic simple shear; nonaffine displacements are differences between endpoints. This correctly strains bonds that cross a periodic face. Pinning the first node removes the same uniform-translation freedom as mean-displacement projection without adding a physical spring.

The stretch residual gradient, signed-cosine residual gradient, shear derivative at fixed nonaffine displacement and sparse Jacobian assembly are algebraically consistent. Duplicate Jacobian entries at a shared junction are coalesced by COO-to-CSR conversion. The stress is the derivative of energy with respect to the imposed nominal shear for the normalized unit reference volume. It is not a measured Cauchy stress or a force in newtons until physical conventions and calibration are supplied.

A deleted segment removes its axial energy and every angle involving it. The fixed-coordinate removal energy therefore includes exactly the deleted axial term and the associated angular terms, with no double counting of angles. Re-equilibration at fixed strain then supplies a distinct relaxation-energy decrement. The linear-system diagonal regularization is used only to obtain a search direction and is absent from the recorded physical energy. Acceptance uses the unregularized gradient.

The independent `audit-math.py` evaluates the equations from inert JSON with Python `math` and checks nine sampled displacement components at three finite-difference steps, the shear derivative, the sum of all node forces and a direct edge-plus-angle deletion identity on each of four geometries. All four pass. At their best finite-difference steps, sampled gradient discrepancies are 1.6e-11–4.1e-11; shear discrepancies are 1.9e-13–2.3e-12. Translation-force sums are below 1.1e-16; edge-deletion energy identity errors are below 5.5e-19. These tests support selected equations and bookkeeping, not the existence of a stable equilibrium or experimental accuracy.

## Material findings and required interpretation

1. **The initial placement comparison did not match its mechanical constraint budget.** Equal selected-junction counts can lock different numbers of intact angles after pruning. At seed 31 and 15% selected sites, the initial clustered set locks 171 active angles while the distributed set locks 181; total angular coefficients are 0.365515 and 0.377721. Similar differences occur in every tested seed. The original series is a site-count-matched diagnostic, not a pure placement effect. A degree-stratified distributed selection matching the clustered counts of degree-2/3/4 nodes holds site count, active-angle count and identical 10× local angular strength simultaneously. An alternative is equal-extra-penalty normalization, but then the per-angle multiplier differs between patterns and must be described accordingly. The parent chose degree stratification for the production design; production arrays need a fresh exact-budget check after regeneration.

2. **Converged is not fractured or fully separated.** Reaching the prescribed shear cap 0.5 may produce no interior maximum and no rupture. An endpoint maximum is right-censored. Report `work_to_cap` separately; call a work-to-peak result observed only with a sampled interior maximum and a subsequent decline. Nonconverged curves are censored at their last accepted state; their partial maxima must not enter complete-case fracture summaries. Damage fraction does not establish loss of periodic load paths. No notched specimen, interface area, physical length mapping or complete separation exists here, so these outputs cannot be called a calibrated fracture toughness Gc.

3. **The original JavaScript and Python peak summaries were inconsistent.** Python includes pre-avalanche stress in the maximum; the original JavaScript summary considered only the post-avalanche value. The production definition should include pre-avalanche stress, and numerical comparison must use matched definitions. Finite step sizes overshoot the first rupture threshold and can alter avalanche size; 100/200-step comparisons are necessary, and large differences must remain visible.

4. **A small gradient does not prove a stable relaxed state.** `JᵀJ` is a Gauss–Newton search matrix and omits residual-weighted second derivatives. In a compressed nonlinear network it can be positive semidefinite even when the true Hessian has a negative eigenvalue. Check true curvature at a documented stratified set of equilibria, especially near peaks/avalanches. A feasible independent check is a central-difference Hessian of the analytic free gradient in diagonally scaled coordinates, symmetrized and evaluated with dense `eigvalsh`. Verify significant negative curvature at a second step size and by directional energy differences. Near-zero modes are marginal/unresolved; no resolved negative mode on sampled states is not universal stability proof. Disconnected components introduce further zero modes after damage; retaining solver regularization in the physical energy would be invalid, and the present code correctly does not do so.

5. **Work–energy accounting must be a numerical result, not assumed.** Compute and publish `W − [U − U0 + fixed-coordinate removal + damage relaxation]`, normalize using a declared robust scale, and screen large errors. Nonlinear branch jumps can dissipate energy during load equilibration even before a segment is deleted; that release is not currently included in damage-relaxation bookkeeping. A large surplus is therefore a warning about unresolved path/snap mechanics, not a license to call it calibrated fracture dissipation. Mesh/time-step qualification cannot be replaced by an energy decomposition identity at one deletion event.

6. **The scalar bovine match is conditional calibration.** Assigning one energy-density factor to each synthetic geometry can match a chosen mean small-strain modulus by construction. It does not identify fiber diameter, physical segment scale, junction stiffness, connectivity, permeability, chemical crosslink density or rupture strain. A parallel bulk dashpot fits macroscopic G″ but does not validate local drag, molecular relaxation or network-rate dependence. The available bovine data do not calibrate aging or fracture. Held-out G′/G″ scores from the phenomenological fit do not independently validate a synthetic 3D strain field or nonlinear stiffening. Measured architecture and withheld nonlinear/local-strain data remain distinct evidence requirements.
