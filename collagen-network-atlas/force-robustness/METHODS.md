# Force concentration and damage spreading across seeds and angles

Design fixed before inspecting results: two architectures (disordered/isotropic and modular), 10 seeds (17,29,43,67,83,101,127,149,173,197), 12 angles (0–165° every 15°), 24 added connectors, two placement patterns, 480/960 steps. There are 480 configurations and 960 solves. The same seed, geometry, collagen volume and constitutive laws are paired. No network mechanics or crosslink law was changed.

Force is evaluated at the last solved strain at or below 0.05, requiring strain ≥0.048 and zero collagen damage. For original collagen fibers only, let w=|axial force|. The effective load-bearing fraction is (sum w)^2 / (N sum w²); lower values indicate greater force concentration. Largest force share is max(w)/sum(w). These describe axial forces rather than stress, exclude connector forces and bending moments, and are sensitive to the prescribed loading boundary. They do not measure the entire force balance at junctions.

Damage is evaluated at the first state with ≥10% original collagen volume ruptured. The damage radius is the volume-weighted radius of gyration of broken-fiber midpoints in the ORIGINAL geometry, divided by the unit-square diagonal. Larger radius means greater spatial extent, not necessarily faster damage propagation. The largest damage-patch fraction is the fraction of broken collagen volume in the largest component of broken fibers sharing original endpoint nodes. Lower patch fraction means more fragmented damage. Damage lying in one connected patch can still have a large radius.

Missing/unconverged measurements are excluded per metric. A ≥5% relative coarse/fine difference flags a metric. For damage metrics, both resolutions must sample between 10% and 15% damage; larger jumps are flagged because the damage stage is poorly matched. Failure censorship does not invalidate an earlier, observed damage-stage measurement. The fine values are displayed, and all coarse/fine values and exclusions remain downloadable. The strain-0.05 sample is approximate, within one load step at each resolution.

Clustered-minus-distributed differences pair seed and angle. Each seed is averaged over its resolved angles, requiring at least six of twelve. The overall mean weights eligible seeds equally. A deterministic 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap resamples seed means, retaining angle dependence within seeds; the displayed 95% interval is exploratory and conditional on resolved cases. Angles are not independent replicates. Positive/negative counts expose angle heterogeneity; they are descriptive, not binomial tests. Complete-case selection may bias contrasts when numerical exclusions differ by architecture or pattern. No multiple-testing adjustment is applied; four correlated metrics are reported together, without searching for an optimal angle.

This evaluates one connector count, fixed hypothetical stiffness 0.20 and rupture extension 0.30 in a small 36-node model. It does not establish robustness across network size, connector chemistry, age, tissue type or arbitrary parameter choices. Single-seed animations illustrate trajectories; these statistics provide the broader comparison. Original authored source uses only Node/Python standard libraries; no cloud expenditure or new dependencies.
