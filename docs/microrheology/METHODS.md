# Larger-network active microrheology pilot

## Executed design

40 paired specimens: disordered/isotropic and modular architectures, 100 nodes (10×10) and 196 nodes (14×14), five seeds (17,29,43,67,83), and distributed versus centrally clustered added crosslinks. Each is measured intact and after a controlled lesion, in x and y probe directions, at Ω = 0,0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3: 960 linear systems. All science code is original and dependency-free, using the existing original model's energy and gradient. No MD, FEniCSx, LAMMPS, new software installation or cloud job was used.

The original geometry builder is extended with n, edge count round(82N/36), lengthBias=(n−1)/5 and target volume. Coordinates and rest lengths are scaled by (n−1)/5 so the reference grid spacing remains 0.2. Collagen area is chosen so the total collagen volume is 0.06 times domain area (unit thickness). Hinge stiffness scales inversely with length, preserving the original EI/L form. The finite domains have side lengths 1.8 and 2.6. The material, nonlinear axial law, bending law and compression response remain the original model. These are statistical family extensions, not tiled copies of the 36-node examples or a converged infinite-domain model.

Added connector count is round(24N/36): 67 or 131. Candidate endpoints are ≤0.46 apart. Connector stiffness is 0.20 times the median original A/L, matching the prior atlas convention; rupture extension is 0.30 but rupture is disabled during the small probe measurement. Distributed sites are seeded randomly; clustered sites are ranked by midpoint distance to the center with small seeded jitter. Central clustering occupies a domain-relative region, so its physical footprint changes between sizes. Length/orientation distributions also differ. Connectors add elastic constraints without changing collagen volume.

## Probe, damage and boundaries

All perimeter degrees of freedom are clamped at rest. There is no imposed prestress. The virtual finite-footprint probe applies total force with equal weights to the four interior nodes nearest (0.35L,0.50L). Its measured displacement is the same weighted average, making drive and readout energy-conjugate. It is not an explicit rigid bead, optical trap, steric contact or slip/binding model.

The lesion removes the three original collagen fibers with midpoints nearest (0.55L,0.50L). These exact edge IDs, the probe and the original skeleton are identical between placement patterns for each size/family/seed. Broken fibers and any hinges requiring them are inactive. Added connectors remain intact. The removed volume and geometry are retained per specimen; removed collagen volume is not identical across architectures/seeds. The lesion is spatially selected, not a random-lesion ensemble. Because it lies near the imposed crosslink cluster, a protective clustered response must not be generalized to all lesion positions.

At rest, the intact and cut networks have negligible gradients, so relaxation does not change the reference coordinates. A central finite difference of the original analytic gradient builds the free-DOF tangent stiffness, at h=10⁻⁶ and h/2. Matrices are symmetrized after recording their asymmetry. Each physical stiffness matrix is normalized by kref, the median undamaged fiber tangent stiffness 0.08A/L. Added-link stiffness is therefore 2.5kref in the small-strain regime. This distinction from the 0.20 A/L multiplier is intentional and inherited from the atlas.

## Harmonic response and validation

The assumed dynamics are uniform nodal drag, C=γI. With exp(iωt) convention, (K/kref + iΩI)u = b, Ω=ωγ/kref. γ and the resulting time scale are hypothetical; Ω is not calibrated to hertz. No solvent hydrodynamics, poroelastic pressure, thermal noise, inertia, crosslink binding kinetics or physical tissue modulus is inferred.

The original complex-symmetric preconditioned iterative solver recomputes the true relative residual. A response requires residual <10⁻⁷; 958/960 pass, with maximum accepted residual below 10⁻⁹. The damaged 196-node modular/distributed seed-43 specimen has a zero-stiffness free motion; both Ω=0 responses are excluded. No stabilizing spring is added. Finite-frequency drag permits its driven responses, while the static matrix remains singular. This is an excluded nonunique global static solution, not a demonstrated thermodynamic transition.

Checks include analytical coupled static/complex solutions, reciprocity, identical paired geometry/probe/lesion, collagen-volume scaling, Hessian-step and symmetry checks, vanishing rest gradient, dissipative sign, and small-amplitude gradient linearization at two amplitudes in both probe directions at Ω=0.1. The maximum component of the tested displacement is 10⁻⁵ (and half that), much smaller than the 0.2 reference grid spacing. The maximum nonlinear-gradient mismatch is about 6.6×10⁻⁵. This is a spatial linearization check, not a time-integration validation or a calibration of drag.

## Reported quantities

Local compliance is |bᵀu| per normalized total force. Remote response is the RMS complex vector displacement over free nodes at distance ≥0.6 (three reference mesh spacings) from the probe centroid. Receivers are identical within intact/damaged pairs, but receiver sets vary between sizes; their RMS is not a size-independent far-field modulus. Radial-bin displacement amplitudes, local phase lag and all response residuals are retained in results.json. Relative changes mean damaged/intact amplitudes; they do not measure a phase-sensitive field difference. Animations additionally show the complex-vector damaged-minus-intact field for one seed.

Statistics pair before/after within a specimen, average the two probe directions per seed, and summarize five seed means with their range. Directions and frequencies are not independent biological replicates. Figures show means across five seeds and two directions, and the table gives seed ranges for Ω=0.1. No multiplicity-adjusted test or universal robustness claim is made. Two sizes reveal sensitivity but do not establish boundary convergence or a long-range decay exponent.
