# Solved force redistribution and damage replay

Four cases: disordered/isotropic and modular networks, seed 17, 24 distributed or clustered added connectors, 0° perimeter loading, 960 strain steps. Model, directional and connector laws are unchanged. `build.js` regenerates solved states with the original authored solver and rejects endpoint metrics differing from the previously published density sweep. `validate.js` independently recomputes every exported fiber/connector force from the saved coordinates and alive flags.

The interactive player exposes all saved equilibria. The 81-frame GIF samples the last solved state at or below each common strain; frames are held without interpolating damage or forces. Each case freezes at its failure criterion. All panels use the same coordinate transform and the maximum absolute force across every fiber, connector and recorded frame. Orange is tension, blue compression; width and opacity encode absolute force. Dashed lines identify added connectors, magenta dashed lines broken elements. Blue nodes are the 20 prescribed perimeter nodes; 16 interior nodes relax. Initial junctions exist at nodes only, not at every drawn line crossing.

Playback speed has no physical-time interpretation. These are equilibrated states after the damage-relaxation loop; instantaneous bond release inside each avalanche is not time-resolved. Collagen damage is the fraction of original collagen volume lost; connector damage is the fraction of added links broken. The GIF samples fewer frames than the interactive/raw export, so a transient sampled state can be skipped in the GIF.

This single-seed comparison illustrates spatial force redistribution and damage progression. It does not establish an architecture-wide ranking, a molecular AGE mechanism, a thermodynamic transition or biological aging calibration. Three-seed density trends and numerical sensitivity flags remain in the parent density data. No new cloud resources or third-party packages were required. `render.swift` uses native macOS graphics frameworks; build and renderer sources are retained.
